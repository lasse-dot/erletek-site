01 — The problem

Construction can't hire its way out of this.

Europe's construction sector needs roughly two million more workers by 2030, driven by renovation targets, climate-adaptation projects, and a workforce that keeps getting older. Demand is rising faster than the industry can staff it.

The work hit hardest is often the least skilled and most physically wearing: pouring long, repetitive runs of concrete wall, by hand, on uneven ground, in the rain. It's the kind of job that stays open even when the pay is decent, because fewer people want to do it.

02 — The machine

Mobile. Modular. Built for real ground.

ERLEtek's printer travels to the job site instead of the job site coming to a factory. It sets up next to the wall it needs to build, reads the terrain underneath it, and prints in place using a vibration-based process that works with both concrete and clay-based mixes.

A human crew still runs the site. The robot takes the part of the job that's long, straight or curved, and repetitive, so skilled workers spend their time on the parts that need judgment.

03 — Beyond Earth

The same machine raises a bigger question.

If a robot can print a wall without a factory around it, reading unfamiliar ground and working with whatever material is on site, it's already halfway to a much harder job: building with regolith, the loose material that covers the Moon's surface, with no crew standing next to it.

ERLEtek is part of ESA BIC Denmark, which backs that parallel track: testing our vibration-printing process on regolith-like material, and researching how it could run with minimal human presence at all. It lines up with ESA's own push toward in-situ resource utilization — building with what's already there instead of shipping material from Earth.

The long-term model

Erle means bee in Basque. Our long-term model is a Robotic Beehive: a swarm of printers building different sections of a structure at once, coordinating digitally so no two machines get in each other's way. It's designed for the Moon, where you can't send a foreman to sort out a scheduling conflict. It works just as well on a job site in Jutland.

In September 2026, ERLEtek presented at ESA's Space for Inspiration event in Copenhagen.
